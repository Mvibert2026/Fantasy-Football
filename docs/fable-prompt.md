## Q1 — Is the simulation-power argument sound? (highest value; it is the PM's own claim)

Read `docs/founder-requests.md` FR-005.

The claim: this project's statistical power problem is not uniform, and depends on the resampling
unit. Season-outcome questions ("does our rank beat consensus", "is there alpha") resample at the
season level — n=4, minimum attainable p ≈ 0.0625 before correction, structurally dead until ~2029.
Draft-mechanics questions ("what does a 15-pick gap cost", "how does slot 3 differ from slot 8")
resample at the *draft* level, and drafts can be simulated without limit, so they are answerable to
tight intervals today. Conclusion drawn: draft-slot simulation should move up the roadmap because it
is the one place rigour produces answers instead of refusals.

**Attack this specifically.** Simulated drafts are generated by our own opponent model, whose
`lambda = 0.352` was fit on a single real draft — n=160 picks, 10 clusters, clustered SE 0.070. A
tight interval around a quantity derived from our own assumptions may be measuring our model rather
than the world. Is the distinction real, or is it laundering assumption into precision?

Also worth pressing: is `lambda`'s clustered SE on 10 clusters even trustworthy? Cluster-robust
variance is known to be biased downward well above that count.

Verdict required: does the simulation route survive, and if so under exactly what conditions?
