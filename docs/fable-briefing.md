# Fable briefing register

**Fable is the weekly outside review — an adversarial consultant, not a workstream.** It runs at the
heaviest available tier, which makes it the most expensive thing this project does. Nothing goes to
it that a cheaper role could answer.

**Standing rule: the founder and PM agree the question list before Fable is invoked.** No question
reaches it unreviewed. This file is where candidates accumulate between sessions.

**Brief it to refute, not to review.** Fable shares a model family with the agents whose work it is
checking, so agreement is cheap and correlated blind spots are the real risk. Give it artifacts
rather than summaries, ask it to argue the opposing case, and require it to justify agreement rather
than defaulting to it. A Fable session that agrees with everything has told you nothing.

**Next session:** the window roughly 3 hours out. It should review the sprint's *actual output* —
commits, thread replies, test counts — not this document's description of it.

## Candidates, ranked

### 1. Is the simulation-power argument correct? — **highest value, and it is mine**
Raised by FR-005. I claimed that the project's statistical power problem is not uniform, and that it
depends on the resampling unit: season-outcome questions resample at the season level (n=4,
structurally unanswerable until ~2029), while draft-mechanics questions resample at the draft level
and can be simulated without limit, making them answerable to tight intervals today. I concluded that
draft-slot simulation should therefore move up the roadmap.

**Why Fable.** This is load-bearing. If the argument is right, it redirects the roadmap toward the
one area where rigour produces answers rather than refusals. If it is wrong — if simulated drafts
inherit enough model dependence that their apparent precision is illusory — then acting on it builds
an expensive capability that produces confident nonsense.

**The specific objection to press:** simulated drafts are generated by our own opponent model, whose
`lambda` was fit on a single real draft. Tight intervals around a quantity derived from our own
assumptions may measure our model rather than the world. Is the distinction I drew real, or am I
laundering assumption into precision?

**Brief:** argue that the simulation route is illusory. Force me to defend it.

### 2. Adversarially check the Strategist's two strong claims
Raised by ADR-A and ADR-B. Both are unusually assertive and both are about to change the product:
- `NEED_ADJUSTMENT_SCALE` is only set-identified — the objective is piecewise constant because the
  parameter enters through an `argmax` — therefore delete it or convert it to a constraint, never fit
  it.
- The pooled rank correlation manufactures correlation from between-position mean differences, so a
  model with zero within-position skill still posts healthy numbers; past figures should be
  **retracted**, not annotated.

**Why Fable.** Both come from a single reasoner with no independent check. Retraction is
irreversible-ish reputationally, and deleting a parameter discards work. Being right matters more
than being fast here.

**Brief:** attempt to refute each. Where is the identifiability argument too strong? Is there a
formulation where the parameter *is* identifiable? Is the pooling critique overstated for this
specific position distribution?

### 6. Where is this operation wasting money? — **founder-requested, and Fable is uniquely placed**
Raised by the founder, 2026-07-26.

The operation now has six agents, pinned model and effort per role, a repo mailbox, a health check,
several generated documents, and a Cowork PM layer on top. None of it has been cost-audited, and it
was all designed by the same party that runs it — which is exactly the conflict an outside consultant
exists to resolve.

**Why Fable specifically.** It is the only reviewer with a view across the whole operation and no
stake in any part of it. Backend cannot judge whether Backend's effort tier is right; I cannot judge
whether the PM layer earns its cost.

**Concrete things to put in front of it:**
- The model/effort table in `docs/operating-model.md`. Is `strategist` at Opus/high justified, or
  would Sonnet at high effort produce the same ADRs? Is `backend` at effort `low` under-powered for
  statistical work, given the project's own history of an under-effort session misreporting?
- The document set. Seven-plus markdown files are now read at session start. Is that already a new
  version of the 52 KB `status.md` problem, rebuilt in a tidier shape?
- The PM layer itself. Roughly 220k tokens ran through Cowork subagents in one day to produce specs
  and one harness. Would the same artifacts have been cheaper produced directly in Claude Code?
  Answer honestly even though the PM commissioned the question.
- The test suite at ~5.7 minutes, dominated by uncached DB-backed archetype tests.
- Whether the mailbox's per-session read cost is repaid by what it saves.

**Brief:** find the waste, and name anything that is ceremony rather than value. Rank by tokens saved
per unit of quality lost — not by tidiness.

### 7. Close the data holes — **the binding constraint on everything**
Raised by the founder, 2026-07-26.

Every genuinely hard blocker in this project is a data problem, not a modelling one. Catalogued:

| Hole | State | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Mock drafts | 1 of ~30 | Availability calibration unvalidated — the product's core claim |
| Per-pick draft state | not captured | `delta` unvalidatable *even with* 30 mocks |
| ADP snapshots | none, none dated | Unrecoverable — a past date's snapshot cannot be reconstructed |
| FantasyPros projections | 40 of 273 players | 233 players have no honest projection |
| Consensus history | 2021–25, one held out | n=4. Every season-outcome test is dead until ~2029 |
| Depth charts | end 2024 | No depth-chart signal for the 2026 draft; blocks `RB_HANDCUFF` |
| Injury status | no `as_of_date` | Historical rebuilds inherit look-ahead |
| Targets / air yards | broken 2003–08 | Features must refuse those seasons |
| DEF scoring | never ingested | DEF has no replacement level, permanently |
| FFC / Yahoo / ESPN / Sleeper ADP | blocked by ToS or auth | No multi-source ADP at all |

**Why Fable.** Individually these are each a small engineering task. Collectively they are a strategy
question nobody has answered: *which holes actually matter, in what order, and which should be
declared permanently closed rather than carried as open items forever?* Some of these have been open
since the project began and are quietly treated as inevitable.

**The specific ask:** rank by consequence-per-unit-effort, and be willing to say "stop tracking this,
it is never getting fixed." Separately — the creative part — find routes nobody has tried. The
project's assumption is that blocked sources stay blocked. Is that true? Is there a legitimate route
to multi-source ADP that has not been considered? Is n=30 mocks actually the right target, or is
there a cheaper design that validates calibration with fewer?

**Note:** thread 009 (source audit) has not yet run. If it completes first, hand Fable the audit
rather than this table. If it has not, this table is the input.

first tends to frame everything afterwards as overhead, and candidates 1, 3 and 7 need it reasoning
about correctness rather than cost.
