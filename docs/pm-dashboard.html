Read this before quoting the product

The signature claim is calibrated availability — who survives to your next pick. It is not calibrated. Zero of roughly thirty real drafts are logged, so there is no evidence a stated 30% happens 30% of the time. Both are honest states, not defects — and both are what the next six weeks are for.

What makes Westwood different

Yahoo's default scoring is already half-PPR, so the receptions difference you might expect isn't there. The real divergence is the stacking yardage bonuses — a player crossing several thresholds collects all of them.

Those bonuses reward ceiling over floor, which should change how variance is valued. And there is an open question, deliberately unanswered, about whether the platform's own rankings price them at all — a threshold bonus depends on single-game distributions, not season totals, so platforms often skip them.

Availability is the differentiator and nothing in the category ships anything like it. It is calibrated on zero drafts. Until mocks are collected and scored, the survival numbers are an honest estimate and should be labelled as one on screen.

The main edge is partly competed awayfinding

Positional revaluation is recorded as the board's only edge channel. Yahoo's paid draft tool uses value-over-replacement — the same family of maths. Against anyone in your league who pays for it, that edge narrows.

Public rooms are standard scoring and standard rosters; yours is three receivers, two flex and no kicker. What transfers is how drafters behave; what does not is how much demand exists per position. Capturing all three scoring formats is what lets that difference be measured rather than assumed.
