# ADR-A — Measuring `NEED_ADJUSTMENT_SCALE`

**Path:** `docs/adr/ADR-A-need-adjustment-scale.md`
**Status:** Proposed
**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Owner:** Strategist (spec) / Backend (execution)
**Test-registry family:** `F-NEEDSCALE`

## Context

`NEED_ADJUSTMENT_SCALE = 10.0` lives in `draft_sim.py` and multiplies the roster-need term inside the recommendation engine, alongside a VBD baseline. It was never measured. It has no units — 10.0 of *what*, relative to a VBD scale whose own units are "points above replacement over a 26-season replacement level" — and no documented derivation. It is currently the single largest unaudited lever in a user-facing number.

Three facts constrain what can honestly be done about it.

**1. The parameter is only set-identified, even with infinite data of this kind.** The scale enters the product exclusively through an `argmax` over a discrete choice set. The objective is piecewise constant in the scale: over any interval where no argmax flips, every value produces byte-identical behaviour. Data can therefore never select a *point* — only the interval containing the best-performing behaviour regime. Any report of a fitted value like "9.4" would be a fabrication of precision. The correct estimand is an interval, and the correct output of a sweep is a partition of the scale axis into behaviour regimes plus a ranking over those regimes.

**2. The evaluation is closed-loop.** Simulated opponents are driven by the availability hazard model, whose roster-need exponent `lambda = 0.352` was fit on **one** draft (n=160 picks, 10 clusters). Tuning our recommender against opponents generated by our own single-draft model measures agreement with our world-model, not skill against real drafters. Separately, a cluster-robust SE on 10 clusters is well below the 30–50 rule of thumb and is biased downward; `z = 5.04` should be re-derived with a wild cluster bootstrap-t before `lambda` is described anywhere as "measured" without qualification. The run term `delta = 0.10` is an admitted unvalidated prior. A sweep of the need scale inherits all of this.

**3. The inference floor is already known to be above the threshold.** Usable non-holdout consensus seasons: **4** (2021–2024). 2025 is sealed. Real logged drafts: **1**. Mocks needed: ~30. Mocks available: **0** — and mocks are judge-only by guardrail, so they can never enter this fit anyway.

## Decision

**Split the question into two, and answer only the one that is answerable.**

### A-1 — Inertness test (answerable now, deterministic, no inference)

*Does the parameter change any decision at all?* This is a property of the code, not of the NFL. It needs no null hypothesis, no CI, no BH correction, and no holdout.

**Sweep values:** `s ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50}`.
Rationale: `0` is the exact null (pure VBD, need term removed); `10` is the incumbent; the rest are log-spaced because the term enters multiplicatively, so linear spacing would waste six of eight points on behaviourally identical territory; `50` is included solely to establish saturation — if behaviour at 50 is indistinguishable from behaviour at 10, the term has already saturated and the incumbent is on a flat part of the curve, which is itself a finding. No value between the grid points is to be added after seeing results; the grid is frozen here.

**Measurement (per `s`, versus `s = 0`):**
- `flip_rate(s)` = fraction of simulated picks where the top-1 recommendation differs from the `s = 0` recommendation.
- `flip_rate_top3(s)` = fraction of picks where the top-3 *set* differs.
- `regime_map` = the sorted list of `s` values at which the top-1 recommendation changes, aggregated across picks — this is the identified partition described above, and it is the honest deliverable.
- All over a fixed, seeded set of simulated drafts: 10 seats × 200 seeds × 4 seasons = 8,000 drafts, seeds recorded. Simulation count here reduces *simulation* noise only; it has no bearing on inference (see A-2).

### A-2 — Superiority test (NOT answerable; do not run as confirmatory)

*Which value drafts better rosters?* Specified fully so it can be executed unchanged in ~2029, and so the power arithmetic is on the record now.

- **Primary metric:** season total of the optimal legal weekly lineup computed from realized 0.5-PPR scores (best-ball scoring), starters QB/RB/RB/WR/WR/WR/TE/FLEX/FLEX/DEF. Chosen because it isolates the draft — it removes waiver activity and start/sit skill, neither of which the assistant controls and both of which would add variance without adding signal. **Known bias, stated up front:** best-ball over-rewards depth and week-to-week volatility relative to a managed league, and therefore biases *toward* larger need adjustments (which broaden the roster). This bias is in the direction of adopting a non-zero scale, i.e. against the conservative decision, which is the right way round for a bias you cannot remove.
- **Secondary metric:** season total under a mechanical lineup rule that sets each week's starters from pre-week consensus projections. Penalizes volatility; biased in the opposite direction.
- **Pre-committed tie-break:** if primary and secondary disagree in *sign*, the result is declared inconclusive. No third metric may be introduced to break the tie.
- **Resampling unit: the season.** Drafts within a season share the same realized player outcomes and are not independent. Bootstrapping over drafts would be pseudo-replication and would produce a CI that shrinks with compute — this is the specific error the alpha-detection review already closed, and it must not reappear here.
- **Contrast:** paired per-season difference vs `bpa_consensus`, and paired vs `s = 0`.

**Power, honestly.** With 4 seasons, the assumption-free floor is a paired sign test: minimum attainable one-sided p is `(1/2)^4 = 0.0625`. That is above 0.05 **before any multiplicity correction, with a single test, at infinite effect size.** With the true family size (7 non-zero sweep values × 2 metrics = 14 confirmatory tests), the smallest attainable BH-adjusted p is `14 × 0.0625 = 0.875`. Even collapsing the family to one primary contrast (`s=10` vs `s=0`, primary metric only) leaves 0.0625. There is no framing of this test that can succeed on 4 seasons. Season-level bootstrap percentile CIs at n=4 are worse than the sign test, not better — the resample space has 256 points, the 2.5% tail is carried by a handful of degenerate all-one-season draws, and nominal coverage is not achieved.

Reaching BH-adjusted 0.05 with a 14-test family under the sign-test floor requires `(1/2)^n × 14 ≤ 0.05`, i.e. **n ≥ 9 usable seasons** — 2021 + 9 = through 2029, with the holdout still reserved. A narrower interval is purchasable with parametric assumptions, but at n=4 those assumptions are untestable, so buying precision that way is buying it from nowhere. This is consistent with, and should be filed under, the existing alpha-detection closure.

## Pre-committed decision rule

Evaluate in order. Stop at the first rule that fires.

**R1 — Parameter is inert → delete it.**
If `flip_rate(10) < 0.02` **and** `flip_rate(50) < 0.05`: the need term does not change recommendations at any plausible magnitude. Remove `NEED_ADJUSTMENT_SCALE` and the need-adjustment code path from the recommendation engine entirely. Do not set it to 0 and leave the branch in place — dead tunable parameters get re-tuned.

**R2 — Parameter is behaviourally live but unidentifiable → convert it to a constraint, not a weight.** *(This is the outcome we expect.)*
If R1 does not fire and A-2 cannot be run (which is the case until n ≥ 9): **do not adopt 10.0, and do not adopt any fitted value.** Instead:
- Reparameterize. The scale is unmeasurable partly because it is unitless. Replace it with a bounded, interpretable quantity `f ∈ [0, 1]`: the 90th-percentile magnitude of the need adjustment, expressed as a fraction of the interquartile VBD gap among currently-available players at that pick. `f` is dimensionless, comparable across positions and rounds, and its endpoints mean something (`f=0` pure VBD, `f=1` need can override a typical VBD gap).
- Set `f` to the **smallest value that satisfies the roster-legality behaviour the term exists to produce**, determined by bisection against a deterministic acceptance test, not by outcome fitting. Proposed acceptance test, to be frozen before running: across all 8,000 simulated drafts, the engine never recommends a 4th player at a position whose starter requirement is already met while an unfilled mandatory slot (QB/TE/DEF) has fewer startable options remaining than picks remaining before the roster closes. Report the bisection result as `f_min`, an interval `[f_min, f_min + grid_step]`, not a point.
- Annotate it in code as a **constraint**, not a fitted parameter. The distinction is the whole point: we are keeping a rule that prevents obviously-broken rosters, and dropping the claim that its magnitude is optimal.

**R3 — Implausibly large effect → treat as a bug.**
If any `s` shows a per-season roster-point difference exceeding 1 SD of the across-season roster-point distribution, do not report it. A draft-time weight tweak producing an effect that large is far more likely a harness defect (lineup optimizer leakage, opponent model degeneracy, seed collision) than a real finding. Route to a harness audit; the sweep result is void until the audit closes.

**No rule adopts 10.0.** The incumbent value can survive only by being inside `[f_min, f_min + grid_step]` after reparameterization, and if it is, it survives as a constraint bound, not as a tuned optimum.

## What "we cannot measure this yet" looks like

**In the codebase.** A parameter registry — `params/registry.yaml` — with one entry per numeric constant that touches a user-facing output. Required fields per entry: `value`, `units`, `provenance ∈ {MEASURED, CONSTRAINT, PRIOR, ARBITRARY}`, `source` (prereg ID, dataset, n, resampling unit), `interval`, `last_reviewed`, `blocked_on`. Initial state:

| parameter | provenance | note |
|---|---|---|
| `replacement_levels` (RB30/WR40/TE10/QB10) | MEASURED | 26 seasons |
| `lambda = 0.352` | MEASURED (qualified) | 1 draft, 10 clusters; CRVE downward-biased; re-derive with wild cluster bootstrap-t |
| `delta = 0.10` | PRIOR | unvalidated, stated as such in docs |
| `NEED_ADJUSTMENT_SCALE = 10.0` | ARBITRARY | → becomes `f`, CONSTRAINT, after R2 |

Enforcement: a CI check that fails the build if any parameter with `provenance: ARBITRARY` is reachable from a code path that renders a number to a user. That check is what makes the registry more than a comment.

**In the UI.** Do not render a need-adjusted score as a number sharing visual weight with VBD — a constraint-derived nudge presented as a decimal is exactly the false-precision failure the competitive research file flags in Fantasy Points' 0–100 prospect composite (a committee judgment wearing a measurement's clothes). Instead:
- Surface the need term as **text, not a delta**: "You still need a TE — 3 startable left." Same idiom as the existing coverage-disclosure pattern ("3 of 10 starters have no projection…"), which that research already identifies as the product's best uncertainty component.
- If a need-adjusted ordering is shown at all, it must be **non-sortable and excluded from the rank column**, the same treatment prescribed for undisclosed ordinal risk scores.
- Methodology surface (Layer 3) carries the registry entry verbatim, including `provenance: CONSTRAINT` and `blocked_on: 9 usable seasons (est. 2029)`.

## Consequences

- The recommendation engine loses a tuning knob and gains a documented constraint. Recommendations become closer to VBD-plus-guardrails, which is more defensible and easier to explain in the "why our rank differs" panel — the panel the research identifies as the direct antidote to the trust problem.
- A-1 is cheap (deterministic, no new data) and gives an immediate, publishable-internally answer.
- A-2 sits in the prereg registry unrun, with its power arithmetic attached, so nobody re-derives the same dead end in eighteen months.
- Risk accepted: if the need term genuinely helps and `f_min` undershoots the optimum, we leave points on the table. We cannot detect this, and pretending otherwise costs more than the points.

## What would falsify this

- **Falsifies R1's premise:** `flip_rate(10) ≥ 0.02` — the parameter is live; R2 governs.
- **Falsifies R2:** the bisection finds no `f` satisfying the acceptance test, i.e. the need term cannot produce roster legality at any magnitude. Then the legality logic belongs in an explicit hard filter on the candidate set, not in a score adjustment, and this ADR is superseded.
- **Falsifies the identifiability argument:** the need term is changed to enter something continuous (an expected-value calculation, a probability) rather than only an `argmax`. Then the objective is no longer piecewise constant and point identification becomes possible in principle — the power problem remains.
- **Falsifies the power arithmetic:** the usable season count rises materially (a validated pre-2021 consensus source is found, or the resampling unit is legitimately argued to be finer than the season — note this argument was already made and rejected once, and reversing it requires reopening the alpha-detection closure, not a side conversation).
