## 1. The strategy selector should collapse — the founder named this one for you himself

Founder's words:

> *"Nice to have strategy selection, but it should be an expandable and shrinkable menu, doesn't have
> to be at the top for every pick - just let me click something and it expands out and pick a
> strategy and shrink it back down."*

It shipped this session as permanent chrome at the top of the Recommend pane. That was right for a
control nobody had seen; it is wrong now that he has.

**The argument for collapsing is not space, it is proportion.** Zero RB tested **NULL** against plain
value-based drafting. The selector reorders recommendations because he may want it to, not because it
wins games — and it says so on screen every time it fires. A control with no measured edge should not
hold permanent space on the screen he stares at for an entire draft.

Two constraints, both non-negotiable:

- **The collapsed state must state the current strategy**, not be an anonymous icon. A reordering he
  has forgotten is active is worse than no reordering.
- **The NULL disclosure survives the collapse.** It must still be seen when the strategy is
  *changed* — not buried behind the expand gesture.

`PANE-LAYOUT-MODES.md` and `PERIODIC-TABLE-GRID.md`'s Expand sheet are both candidate mechanisms you
already built. Borrowing beats inventing.

## 2. The archetype chip — a measurement that may send you back to `PLAYER-PROFILE.md` §4

You designed §4 around a label that is *frequently* weak or absent. Measured live against the
shipping export, it is worse than that:

| Covered-position board rows (RB/WR/TE) | 460 |
|---|---|
| Carry a real archetype label | 194 — **42.2%** |
| Render `UNCLASSIFIED` | 266 — **57.8%** |

**The no-chip card is the normal layout, not the fallback.** The ~34% figure in the earlier brief
came from a researcher's snapshot; this is what the browser actually loads.

Two consequences worth designing to:

- If the identity strip's spacing assumes a chip, it reflows on three cards in five. The common case
  should be the layout without it.
- Your rule still holds and is now better supported — a chip present on 42% of players is exactly the
  case where an honest absence beats a forced bucket.

**Separately, the founder wants the taxonomy itself rebuilt** (FR-123): *"the archetpes need work, we
probably need more categories - the 35% rule can't be global… maybe we need one in football terms and
one in fantasy context/terms."* That is a modelling job before it is a design job and it is **not
yours this round** — but if a two-axis label is where it lands (how a team uses him / what owning him
does to a roster), the identity strip has to survive carrying two things. Worth knowing before you
finalise it.

## 3. Injury and availability status at a glance — spec the target, the data is not built

Founder's words:

> *"injury status should show in lists of players and in player card, if no injury, it should show
> healthy - needs to be easy to see at a glance - this includes suspension or IR, or PUP etc but all
> the regular status as well"*

**The data does not exist yet.** `board.json` carries no injury status at any granularity — no IR, no
PUP, no questionable/doubtful/out. `roster_status` is a *contract* field with three values, and
`suspension_flag` is `False` for all 510 players. Sourcing is queued behind the frontend work.

**Spec it anyway, because the placement question is yours and it is contested.** Two surfaces:

- **In lists.** `RANKINGS-PANE.md` defines a strict column drop order. A status indicator has to enter
  that order explicitly. It drops before the player's name, which never drops — where else?
- **On the card.** `PLAYER-PROFILE.md` §1 puts identity first. Status belongs in the identity strip —
  the same line the archetype chip wanted. **Status would be present on 100% of players; the archetype
  chip on 42%.** If both cannot fit, say which wins and why.

**One rule the design must encode, not just respect.** "If no injury, show healthy" is the dangerous
half of the ask. Absence of an injury record is not evidence of health. Whatever you spec, the healthy
state must be renderable *only* from a source that affirmatively lists a player as available — and
there must be a distinct fourth state for "not tracked," which is what every player is today. Three
states where there should be four is how this ships wrong.

## 4. The news feed — what to spec now, and what not to

Founder's words: *"News feed (global), and per player (leading to roster specific) needs to start -
this will be useful for drafts"*. He has raised it three times.

**Blocked on a source**, which is a research question in flight. Do not design a feed's content rules
before anyone knows what content is available.

**What is worth your time now** is the one thing that will be wrong if it is retrofitted: **an empty
news panel because ingestion failed looks identical to an empty news panel because nothing happened.**
Under the never-fabricate rule those must read differently. This is the most likely place in the whole
app for it to silently mislead him on draft morning. Spec the empty states — all of them — and leave
the populated state until the source is known.

## 5. One thing item 8 needs from you — a container, not a rewrite

Backend specified the honest caption for `vs your options`, and the wording is right:

> *"…this player's value against the best other player available right now for the same roster spot,
> given your picks so far. It does not change the ranking above. This project tested whether reasoning
> about flex/alternative value produces better draft outcomes than plain positional value
> (test-registry #35, #36) and found no measurable edge at the current sample size (n=4 seasons) —
> treat this number as a way to see where the two questions disagree, not as a stronger
> recommendation."*

**It is ~70 words and it was specified to land on a header tooltip.** That breaks your own limit from
`PROVENANCE-DISCLOSURE.md`: hover carries one short human sentence, ~12 words, never a paragraph — for
your stated reasons, all of which apply here.

Frontend has been told to split it your way: one sentence on hover, the full text behind *Why that
matters*, alongside the ADP caveat. **The NULL disclosure is not shortened and not dropped — it moves
one gesture.** Confirm that is the right container, or specify a better one.
