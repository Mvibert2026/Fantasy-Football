## Request

> "Ok, but again we need everything consensus has to get our own rankings up to snuff."

**This is a different instruction from anything else on the backlog and it should not be collapsed
into the edge programme.**

## What he is correcting

The campaign has been organised around **finding an edge** — what does consensus not know. He is
pointing at the prior question: **what does consensus already know that we do not?**

Those are not the same problem and only one of them is currently blocking.

**Consensus is a fully-informed board.** It prices coaching changes, camp reports, contract news,
depth-chart movement, beat-writer signal, injury recovery timelines and scheme fit. **v1 has none of
that.** Its inputs are age, prior-year share, injury designations and depth-chart role, several of
which measured null at ranking level.

So v1 does not lose to consensus because consensus has a subtle edge. **It loses because consensus
knows things v1 has never been told.** That is a parity problem, not an edge problem, and it explains
the measured result better than any factor hypothesis tested this week:

| | |
|---|---|
| v1 vs consensus | loses at 3 of 4 positions, BH-significant |
| v1 vs prior-season points | **wins** decisively at RB and WR |
| v1 vs tier heuristic | **wins** decisively at RB and WR |

**v1 beats the naive baselines and loses to the informed one.** That is the signature of a model with
sound machinery and missing inputs — not one with a broken method.

## Why this reorders the work

**A factor that is "already priced into consensus" is not worthless to us — it is required.** The
campaign has been discarding exactly those, on the reasoning that they cannot produce an edge. For a
board that starts *from* consensus, that reasoning holds. **For a bottom-up model built from zero, it
is backwards**: those factors are the floor it has to reach before an edge is even a coherent
ambition.

This also reframes the ~90 nulls. They were measured as *"does this improve our model"* against a
primary that already inherits consensus. Tested instead against a from-zero model, several could be
load-bearing rather than redundant. **Nothing here says to re-run them; it says the earlier reading was
narrower than it sounded.**

## The sequence this implies

1. **Parity first.** Enumerate what consensus prices that v1 cannot see, and close the gaps that are
   reachable from data already held. Related but distinct: `FR-2026-07-30-bottom-up-must-include-table-stakes`
   (twelve Tier-0 factors, four built) and the availability defect the founder found today.
2. **Then edge.** Threshold and interaction forms
   (`FR-2026-07-31-threshold-tests-as-a-class`) and residual discovery
   (`FR-2026-07-31-reverse-discovery`) are the right instruments — **after** the model can see what the
   crowd sees.

**Testing for edge from a model that lacks parity measures the wrong thing.** It has been measuring
the wrong thing all week, honestly and expensively.
