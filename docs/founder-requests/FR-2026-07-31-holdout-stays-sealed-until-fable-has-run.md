## Ruling

> "We won't unlock the holdout until after fable has a chance to run."

## Why this is the right order, stated so nobody re-opens it

**The holdout is the only non-renewable thing this project owns.** It can be spent once. Every
season before 2025 can be re-analysed indefinitely; 2025 answers exactly one question, once, and
then it is training data forever.

**Spending it before adversarial review means spending it on whatever the project believed at the
time.** Today alone, review overturned three things that would otherwise have been carried into a
holdout test:

- The "12-of-12 zeros prove the metric is blind" framing — **one algebraic identity printed twelve
  times**, knowable with no data at all.
- Four arms reported as "improvements the consensus bar suppressed" — **`E1b`/`E2` never compared
  against consensus**; two of the four measured as degradations.
- The oracle ladder's headline — the games-played oracle **shares its zero set with the outcome**.

Any of those could have justified opening 2025 on Tuesday's understanding. All three were wrong by
Wednesday.

## The rule

1. **No agent opens the 2025 holdout on its own authority** — including on a result it considers
   decisive. It stops and escalates to the founder.
2. **fable runs first.** Its mandate (`docs/fable-mandate-M-2026-07-29.md`) is written, targets the
   three model questions and the campaign's audit trail, and has never been run.
3. Every access is logged in `docs/preregistration/holdout_access_log.jsonl` — 19 entries and counting.

Written into `CLAUDE.md` §6.3 so it is read at session start rather than depending on this file being
found.

## Note on cost, since PM has got it wrong twice

fable is **accounted separately but draws on the main pool and counts against the weekly total**
(founder's correction, 2026-07-30). Gating the holdout on fable is a real spend decision, not a free
one, and it is his to time.
