## Request
Fable work happens at the end of the week before the budget reset, not mid-week

> "what are you doing with fable right now, we don't need to do that work now, I do fable work at
> the end of the week before the reset?"

Founder's own words, 2026-07-29, on seeing three Fable mandates dispatched on a Wednesday.

## Why it matters

Fable draws on a **separate weekly budget** that the founder spends deliberately at the end of the
week, immediately before it resets. Spending it mid-week either wastes budget he had allocated
elsewhere or leaves the end-of-week slot empty.

Nothing in the repo recorded this. `docs/operating-model.md` said "Weekly" and `docs/pm/ROLE.md`'s
cadence table said "Weekly — a Fable mandate carrying the standing question," neither of which
distinguishes *which day*. A new PM reading those documents would make exactly the mistake that was
made: reading "the M mandate has never been run" as an urgent gap and dispatching it immediately.

## Initial read

**Recorded in `docs/pm/ROLE.md`** in two places — the cadence table and the "Managing Fable"
section — as a founder constraint rather than a scheduling preference.

The operating rule that follows: **write mandates whenever the question arises, queue them, and run
the queue at the end of the week.** Writing a mandate is cheap and is best done while the context is
fresh; running one is what costs. Do not dispatch mid-week because something looks urgent — on
2026-07-29 the PM launched M-1, M-2 and M-3 simultaneously on a Wednesday and the founder stopped
all three. They were killed while still reading and produced no output, so nothing was lost beyond
the tokens.

**Currently queued and unrun:** the three M mandates (`docs/fable-mandate-M-2026-07-29.md` — the
founder's three model questions, his stated condition for using the tool in the real draft);
G-B, G-D and G-E (`docs/fable-mandate-G-2026-07-28.md`); K-A, K-B, K-C and K-D
(`docs/fable-mandate-K-2026-07-28.md`); and F-B, F-C, F-D
(`docs/fable-mandate-2026-07-28-short.md`). G-C is folded into M-2 rather than run separately.

That is a large queue for one end-of-week slot, and choosing which subset runs is a PM decision that
should be made deliberately rather than by running whatever is nearest. **K-A must not be framed by
the PM** — it reviews a claim the PM authored.
